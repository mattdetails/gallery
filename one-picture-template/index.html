One Picture: Vision Template

2026-10-07

One vision, one page, read in 60 seconds: seven slots that take a big idea from scattered to focused.

The seven slots

Slots 1, 4 and 7 are the busy-reader version; the rest is for anyone who reads on.

#SlotWhat it saysLimit
1HeadlineWhat changes, for whom, by when1 sentence
2Why nowWhat shifted, and the cost of staying put2–3 sentences
3The pictureOne concrete scene from the future where it worked3 sentences
4The betThe one choice it rests on, and what you won't do2 sentences
5Three movesThe steps, in order3 lines
6How we'll knowSigns you can see or count1–3 signs
7The askWhat you need from this reader1 sentence

How to write one

  1. Dump. Write down every idea, unfiltered. A voice note or meeting transcript works.

  2. Sort. Put each idea into one slot. Anything that fits nowhere goes on a Later list: kept, just not in this story.

  3. Write. Fill slots 2 to 7, then write the headline last and put it first.

  4. Cut. Remove any sentence the reader wouldn't miss. A first draft usually loses a third.

  5. Test. Read it aloud. Over 60 seconds, trim Why now and The picture first.

Rules that keep it focused

  • One vision per page. Two headlines means two pages.

  • Never more than three moves.

  • Always say what you won't do; it is what makes the page read as intentional.

  • Concrete beats abstract: a person, a day, a number.

  • The headline and the ask are never cut.

Blank page to copy

# [Vision name]

**Headline.** [What changes, for whom, by when. One sentence.]

**Why now.** [What shifted. What staying put costs. Two or three sentences.]

**The picture.** [One scene from the future where this worked: who, doing what, what's different.]

**Three moves.**

Worked example

An invented example, loosely based on Graphyte: the plans, dates and figures below are made up for illustration. The dump had twelve ideas; five went to Later.

Headline. By the end of 2027, every Graphyte project ships at a fixed price, in weeks rather than months, built by AI agent teams with me as the only human in the loop.

Why now. Clients no longer trust hourly billing and won't wait three months for a build. Over the past year, agent teams went from experiment to doing most of our build work. Studios that keep selling hours will lose on price and speed.

The picture. A founder signs a quote on Monday. By Wednesday her board is set up and engineers are building. Every Friday she sees what moved; six weeks later she launches, at the price she signed.

The bet. The real limit is my attention, not engineering capacity, so every process protects it. Not doing: growing a human team, building our own software product, or taking hourly work.

Three moves.

  1. Q4: one operating system across every project: one set of rules, one board standard, one escalation path.

  2. Q1: three fixed-price offers with set scope: a site rebuild, an app's first version, a growth sprint.

  3. Q2: double the number of clients with no new hires.

How we'll know. Every project lands at its quoted price. The typical project launches in under 8 weeks. My hours per project keep falling.

The ask (for an advisor). Pressure-test the three offers before Q1, and introduce me to two founders who would buy the first one.

Busy-reader version: Every Graphyte project will ship at a fixed price in weeks, built by AI agent teams. The bet: my attention is the limit, so no new hires, no SaaS, no hourly work. I need your eyes on three offers and two founder intros.

Later: a SaaS, a course for other agencies, a rebrand, a podcast, narrowing to Webflow and FlutterFlow.
